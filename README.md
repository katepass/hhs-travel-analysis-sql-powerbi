# Travel Pattern Analysis of Women in Marshall County, IA
This project is being completed while I am a Data Analysis Intern in Maternal and Child Health Epidemiology with the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services.

## Background
As of 2024, at least 41 hospitals in Iowa have closed their obstetrics units since 2000, leaving 57% of Iowa counties without birthing services. In September 2019, UnityPoint Health in Marshalltown was the 34th hospital in Iowa to close its obstetrics unit, leaving Marshall County without birthing services. UnityPoint Health reported a 45% decrease in births between 2015 and 2019 as women chose to give birth at larger hospitals located 30 to 50 miles away. Since the closure, pregnant women in the area must travel at least 30 miles from Marshalltown to the nearest birthing center. While some women in Marshall County were already traveling to other hospitals before the closure, it is unknown how travel patterns shifted after the local option was unavailable.

## Study Question
How did travel patterns and birth locations for pregnant women in Marshall County change between 2015 and 2025, both before and after the 2019 closure of UnityPoint Health in Marshalltown?

## Dataset Questions
- How many babies were born at the hospital each year? (2015-2025)
- Which zip codes/cities were making up the births by year? How did the demographics change as Marshalltown approached closure?
- Of the primary communities/zip codes served by Marshalltown, how many people were already choosing another hospital (Ames/DSM) before the closure?
- What is the average/range of travel times to the next closest hospital?
- Which zip codes serviced by Marshalltown have the furthest drive to a birthing center?
- Which hospital(s) are the communities/zip codes previously served by Marshalltown choosing to go to now?
- What is the correlation between distance to give birth and no prenatal care received?
